# A Work of “Pure Fiction”

## A Trans Woman at the Gates of the Temple of TERF

Incense smoke curled before the Temple of TERF. High upon her throne sat Her TERFiness, close-cropped and solemn, an XX-shaped scepter in her hand.

“Have you, too, come to join the faith?”

“The one-drop rule. One drop of Black blood, and you were Black. White people counted blood that way for centuries. So tell me: are you a pure-blood woman?”

“The unclean may not enter the temple. If you insist on coming in, you must accept your place as a pariah. Your body, your mind, your politics—none will escape inspection. One trace of maleness, and you are nothing but filth: tainted by privilege, harboring wicked thoughts, destined to bring calamity.”

I lowered my head. I had come from far away, over mountains and across rivers, shameless enough to call the journey a “transition.” I wanted to rid myself of everything that had come before, simply because too much pain lived there. Pain had spared none of it: body, mind, politics.

When the people of the land where I lived discovered that the world I longed for was not quite the world they wanted, they offered me two choices: correction or exile.

I packed my things and went in search of a sage.

“You have been wounded by the Pattyarchs,” the sage told me. “Their crimes are without number. Long ago, others wounded as you were journeyed to the far end of every road, where the mountains close in and the waters run dry, and founded the Feminia Autonomous Zone.

“People of different cultures, languages, and classes have raised their camps there. Should fortune carry you that far, perhaps you will find salvation among our communities in exile.”

The sage also told me:

“The moment you name yourself, you become what you name. Identity is essence.”

Most Feminians believed some version of this. The Pattyarchs had ruled Feminians with the word “essence” for so long that the word itself had grown heavy with history.

Beep. Beep. Beep.

The frantic sound of a machine tore me out of the memory.

“Essentially, the results are already quite clear…”

Her TERFiness’s voice hovered above the crown of my head.

“You are guilty. You can never wash away your origins. Women are clean. Women are moral. WOMEN ARE BORN GOOD.

“But you—you are only an immigrant. The defects of your stock were written into your genes long ago.”

I believed all this too—or at least the middle part.

I was enchanted by women. I adored them; I revered them. In the society I had known, the questions that gathered around women were the first to make me feel like a citizen.

When I became certain that I did not belong to the land assigned to me, I also answered the call of so many independent, strong, and powerful women. I fell in love with everything that belonged to women.

The sage had told me that to name oneself was to be saved.

So should I believe them?

At least most of the women I had seen, the women around me, were clean and moral in their hearts. I would silently repeat the slogan to myself—WOMEN ARE BORN GOOD—and imagine that we were living in another timeline, one where the Feminians of the seventies had invented another brilliant game of words and sent the Pattyarchs spinning in circles.

How could I possibly not belong here?

I identified with women, and I adored women. After registering in Feminia, I had even made plans to visit the promised land called Lez-La Land!

But I truly hated the origins assigned to me. I longed for belonging above all else. Without belonging, I would never feel safe.

Feminia seemed like my final hope. As the sage had said, the Pattyarchs were still driving me and others like me from place to place. Even the Pattyarch subjects who claimed neither to encourage nor oppose our existence—and those who could no longer even be bothered to pretend—chased after us like hungry ghosts, stones already raised in their hands.

“Please. Let me enter Feminia. I will give everything.

“I will lay my body, my will, and my courage upon the altar. I will give my money to those sharpening their knives. Let them stitch me, cut me, carve me, shape me—and then cleave in two the continuous life that should have been mine.”

“Ha, ha, ha! Drunk on stereotypes, objectifying women, dissolving female solidarity, rotten from birth—and you still deny that you are a familiar of the Pattyarchs?”

I rubbed my eyes.

Had I taken a wrong turn somewhere?

This level of theory gets you a throne now?

But I was so lonely.

I longed so desperately to be received into the fold. I wanted so badly to belong to women’s shared world of feeling.

What was that term the sage had stuffed into my pocket again?

Oh, right.

The **affective commons**.

Wait, yes—wasn’t this supposed to be a commons?

Then what was this Temple of TERF doing here?

Who had built a temple on the commons?

And why, on this supposedly common ground, was everyone still performing a grand tribute ceremony to the Cis-Cis and the Hetty-Hets?

Her TERFiness laughed too hard. For one careless instant, her mask slipped and clattered to the floor.

Behind it were bared and crooked fangs, nothing like the Feminians I had heard about.

I understood at last.

I turned and ran, slipping out of that airless world an instant before the temple doors slammed shut.

I planted my flag where I stood.

Before long, a band of Feminians gathered around me. Around my flag they planted the emblems of Lez-La Land, Queerling Wanderers, Anti-Essies, Marxie-Femmies, Posties, Intersecties, and Black Femmies.

A fire was lit, and we gathered around it.

I had not felt such warmth in a very long time.

This, perhaps, was what it meant to be received into the fold.

But would I be allowed to remain?

Artillery fire came from the right of the camp.

Black clouds bore down upon the city walls as if to crush them.

Wait—seriously?

Another TERF goddess over there, razing camps and storming strongholds?

The goddess wore a witch’s robes and waved a wand as she chanted. Behind her, a troop of Ministry of Magic lackeys chanted along:

“What an ungrateful little actress, daring to defy me. Do not forget: the Pattyarch press will speak for me too. I am morality itself.”

“No one loves women more than I do. Look—I drove some of those who came here away. Now we shall be pure forever, safe forever.

“Come, everyone. Let’s find out whether she has *that thing*…”

“Happy International Fake Oppression Day to everyone who needs strangers to know they don’t want sex…”

The shelling from the right grew fiercer.

I closed my eyes and prayed devoutly for a better tomorrow.

Boom, boom, boom.

Bang.

Aah!

Resist!

Duck!

Survive!

Then I woke with a start.

It had only been a dream.

My roommate was already up. She said she, too, had received an oracle in her sleep.

It consisted of a single line:

**Don’t play with the TERFy piggies. Let them stomp their little feet all by themselves.**
